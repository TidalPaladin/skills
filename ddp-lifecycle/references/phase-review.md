# DDP phase review reference

Use this reference after you confirm the current approved DDP and risk-management procedures. The requirements below summarize approved `MC-000432-PR`, version 3, and `MC-000430-PR`, version 1, as checked on August 27, 2026.

The procedures are the source of truth. Recheck them before you rely on this reference for a current release.

## Regulatory cross-reference

The Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference. QMSR 21 CFR 820.10(c) requires Clause 7.3 and its subclauses for applicable devices.

QMSR and ISO 13485 do not require one named risk-review form at each phase. They require a documented risk-based process. `FRM RAF-01` is the Phase 3 manifestation of the MedCognetics process. It does not replace risk work in other phases.

### Phase 0

- ISO 13485:2016: 4.1.2, 7.1, and 7.3.1.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(a) and 820.10(c).
- Risk review: Define the product, intended use, trigger, initial hazards, risk plan, and roles.

### Phase 1

- ISO 13485:2016: 7.2.1, 7.2.2, 7.3.2, 7.3.4, and 7.3.9.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(c).
- Risk review: Link hazards and controls to complete, unambiguous inputs and planned verification.

### Phase 2

- ISO 13485:2016: 4.2.3, 7.3.3, 7.3.4, and 7.3.9.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(c) and 820.45 for applicable labeling and packaging controls.
- Risk review: Confirm that outputs implement controls and planned tests can verify them.

### Phase 3

- ISO 13485:2016: 7.3.4, 7.3.5, 7.3.6, and 7.3.9.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(c).
- Risk review: Use `FRM RAF-01` to assess system risks, test controls, decide residual risk, and assign action-sheet items.

### Phase 4

- ISO 13485:2016: 4.2.3, 4.2.5, 7.3.7, and 7.5.1.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(c), 820.35, and 820.45.
- Risk review: Confirm final controls, traceability, residual-risk decisions, and feedback mechanisms before transfer.

### Phase 5

- ISO 13485:2016: 7.3.8, 8.2.1, 8.2.2, 8.4, 8.5.2, and 8.5.3.
- QMSR: 21 CFR 820.10(b)(3), 820.35, and 21 CFR part 803 for applicable reportable complaints.
- Risk review: Assess signals and trends. Update risk management when a signal changes risk or causes a design change.

The QMSR cross-reference is not a substitute for a current regulatory applicability assessment. Check device classification, market, and applicable FDA requirements before you make a compliance conclusion.

## Proof of concept

The optional proof-of-concept path permits limited experimental, proof-of-concept, or basic trial work. It must have Executive Approval before it starts. The work must remain traceable and must not add product features before the full DDP begins.

## Phase 0: project planning

Purpose: Decide if the organization will start a controlled development project.

Minimum evidence:

- Project Design and Development Plan with phase deliverables.
- Documented risk discussion and initial risk plan, risk register, hazard analysis, or FMEA path.
- Use case when Marketing provides one.
- Completed Phase Development Plan form for the phase.
- Executive Authorization to proceed to Phase 1.

Review the plan for the product concept, intended user, intended use, responsibilities, phase deliverables, interfaces, and phase-entry criteria. Verify that the risk discussion identifies project, company, and user issues. Verify that it moves applicable issues into the risk-management work.

Risk review evidence: Record a risk discussion. Create or identify the initial risk-management artifacts. Identify initial hazards, hazardous situations, and foreseeable misuse. Create a separate risk-review record when the project initiation decision includes a documented risk decision.

Exit: Executive Authorization starts Phase 1.

## Phase 1: design input

Purpose: Define complete, testable design inputs before design output work starts.

Minimum evidence:

- Dated and approved User Needs Requirements document with intended use and unique requirements.
- Design Inputs document that transfers User Needs by number.
- Product Requirements document with unique identifiers.
- Separate, numbered, approved, and dated Software Requirements document.
- Initial Hazard Analysis and FMEA, based on the project Risk Plan.
- Regulatory Strategy.
- V&V Plan.
- Traceability Matrix.
- Updated Phase Development Form with document numbers and activity evidence.
- Cross-functional design-review sign-off before the next phase.

Review the documents for one requirement per identifier, complete and non-conflicting inputs, stated intended use, risk-control requirements, and traceability to planned verification. Verify that the design review rejects incomplete, ambiguous, or conflicting requirements.

Risk review evidence: Review the initial Hazard Analysis and FMEA at the design-input decision point. Confirm that risk controls become traceable design inputs. Create a risk-review record when the team decides on risk ratings, controls, or risk acceptability.

Exit: The cross-functional design review signs off before Phase 2 work starts.

## Phase 2: design output

Purpose: Define design outputs that meet the design inputs and prepare the product for V&V.

Minimum evidence:

- Product specifications that support intended use, safety, and performance.
- Updated Hazard Analysis and FMEA with mitigations and updated ratings.
- Updated V&V Plan, including the required test phases.
- Approved test plans and reports for each completed test activity.
- Approved draft labeling.
- Draft DHF, DHR, and DMR traceability evidence.
- Updated Traceability Matrix.
- Updated Phase Development Form.
- Phase design-review evidence and sign-off before V&V.

Review the outputs for traceable coverage of every input. Check that test activities have a separate approved report. Check that risk controls appear in the design and test planning. Check that labeling and lifecycle records are ready for V&V work.

Risk review evidence: Review the updated Hazard Analysis and FMEA when mitigations and revised ratings are decided. Confirm that design outputs implement risk controls and test planning can verify them. Create a risk-review record for that risk decision.

Exit: The phase evidence shows that the design is ready for V&V.

## Phase 3: verification and validation

Purpose: Verify design inputs and validate that the product meets User Needs and intended use.

Minimum evidence:

- Completed, reviewed, and approved V&V protocols with unique test-case identifiers.
- Final V&V Plan.
- Results document for each test plan, with acceptance, rejection, or justified exceptions.
- Updated Traceability Matrix with test-case coverage.
- Completed `FRM RAF-01` System Risk Assessment.
- Risk Assessment Action sheet for hazards that need further control.
- Cross-functional design-review and Milestone 3 sign-off.

Review the protocols for approval before use and independence from their author. Check that every result has an explicit outcome. Check the Traceability Matrix for coverage of all requirements. Check that the System Risk Assessment documents controls and moves uncontrolled risks to the action sheet.

Risk review evidence: Complete `FRM RAF-01`. Review the system risks and controls. Verify the risk-control evidence. Validate control effectiveness when applicable. Record a risk-review decision for residual or uncontrolled risks.

Exit: Milestone 3 sign-off confirms that the design is ready for design transfer.

## Phase 4: production and design transfer

Purpose: Complete the design and transfer the product to controlled production.

Minimum evidence:

- Completed Traceability Matrix, or written justification for each remaining unmet requirement.
- Risk Management Report with final Hazard Analysis and FMEA results and mitigations.
- Product Medical Device File with the required product description, intended use, instructions for use, specifications, manufacture and distribution procedures, and installation and service procedures.
- Design-transfer documentation with component specifications, top-level specifications, assembly procedures, and labeling specifications.
- Design-review and Milestone 4 sign-off.

Review the transfer package for complete requirements coverage and final risk controls. Verify that the Risk Management Report includes the risk-management plan status, risk matrices, control effectiveness traceability, residual-risk evaluation, risk-benefit analyses, and post-production feedback mechanisms.

Risk review evidence: Review the Risk Management Report and residual-risk decisions. Confirm that risk controls are traceable to the transfer package. Confirm that feedback mechanisms can identify new risks. Create a risk-review record for the design-transfer or residual-risk decision.

Exit: Milestone 4 sign-off confirms complete design documentation and controlled transfer readiness.

## Phase 5: post-market surveillance

Purpose: Monitor product quality and effectiveness after shipment.

Minimum evidence:

- Records that monitor and record issues that can affect product quality or effectiveness.
- Monthly complaint-monitoring records for the first distribution year, including identified trends and recurring problems.

Review the surveillance evidence for the product, applicable period, signal trend, assessment, and action. Do not treat a surveillance plan as completed surveillance.

Risk review evidence: Review complaint, cybersecurity, service, change, and corrective-action information for risk signals. Update risk work when a signal changes the risk profile. Create a risk-review record for the resulting risk decision.

Exit: Phase 5 continues while the product remains active. There is no one-time completion gate.

## Form and record selection

`FRM RAF-01` is the System Risk Assessment form for Phase 3. The approved procedure does not identify one identical risk-review form for every other phase.

For Phase 0 through Phase 4, identify the current approved phase form, risk artifact, or risk-review record in Cognidox. Use the project-specific Phase Development Form to record phase activity and evidence. Do not use an old completed product record as a blank template.
