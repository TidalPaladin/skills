# Prose Editing Patterns

Use these patterns to find unclear or repetitive writing. They do not establish
whether a person or model wrote the text. Do not use statistical thresholds or
model stereotypes to infer authorship.

## Concrete Meaning

Replace vague claims with supported facts. Remove intensifiers that add no
information. Preserve uncertainty when evidence is incomplete or disputed.

| Pattern | Revision |
| --- | --- |
| "It is worth noting that the test failed." | "The test failed." |
| "The system has a significant impact." | State the measured effect, or omit the claim. |
| "Due to the fact that..." | "Because..." |
| "A comprehensive solution..." | Name the capability or change. |

Do not invent a measurement to make a sentence more specific.
Attribute a claim only to a source that supports it.

## Structure and Tone

Use headings that name the content. Put related facts together and vary structure
when the subject requires it. Avoid repeated conclusions, empty transitions,
rhetorical questions, dramatic metaphors, and unnecessary contrast.

Lists and tables are useful for parallel items. Do not force prose into a
template or alter sentence lengths to satisfy an authorship heuristic.

## Terms and Safe Zones

Use technical words for their precise meanings. A word such as "ecosystem" can
be appropriate when it names a defined system. Judge it in context.

Preserve quotations, titles, names, commands, code, identifiers, and mandatory
wording. Remove stray generated markup from ordinary prose, but retain literal
examples that explain such markup.

## Review

Read the result for meaning and flow. Check that:

- Every factual claim has support.
- Each paragraph develops a clear topic.
- Repetition serves the reader.
- Headings describe their content.
- Uncertainty matches the evidence.
- Technical text follows [ASD-STE100 guidance](asd-ste100.md).

Do not claim formal ASD-STE100 compliance from a pattern scan.
